TD3 measurements: choreography on open ground

Measured on Cygnus (Windows 11, .NET SDK 10.0.403, x64) on 2026-10-27, while another agent
session ran Unity on the same machine (10-20% of total CPU busy), so absolute times carry noise.
Commands run from the worktree's root.

Files
- tick-timing.md: build time and sim tick timing for the song and for S1-S5 at N = 500, 1000,
  2000 and 2800 at a 40 m view:
    dotnet run --project demo3d/dotnet/Demo3D.Choreography.Cli -c Release -- timing
- song-summary.txt: the song's per-section table (... -- song).
- song-dump-37.0-37.6.json: the debug dump for 37.0-37.6 s (break-1 into drop-1's first
  downbeat): ... -- song --out <file> --from 37.0 --to 37.6. The whole dump is about 81 million
  characters, so it is not kept.

The song's build time (budget: under 3 s)
  CLI, Release                                        first 1.51-1.56 s, second 1.52 s
  dotnet test (Debug; the layer has Optimize=true)    first 1.25-1.38 s, second 1.03-1.24 s
  CLI, Debug with -p:Optimize=false                   first 2.62 s, second 2.55 s
  Unity 6.6 editor EditMode test (Mono, editor code)  first 6.93-6.96 s, second 6.61-6.98 s
The Unity row comes from the two cold `demo3d check` runs; it is not
the budget, since the editor runs Mono with debug code. The test asserts 3 s under .NET and a
20 s sanity bound on Mono. A player build (IL2CPP) was not timed.

Sim tick timing (one thread, 60 Hz, first 5 s excluded; tick-timing.md)
- Song: p50 0.015 ms, p99 2.9-3.0 ms, max 14-18 ms, mean 0.12-0.13 ms over 11,640 ticks.
- Stress at 40 m: p99 about 0.6-0.8 ms at N = 500, 1.4-1.8 ms at 1000, 3.2-4.2 ms at 2000,
  3.7-6.0 ms at 2800 (S1-S5).
- Every tick advances every monster. Beat ticks (1 in 23 at 155 bpm, 1 in 26 at 140 bpm) also
  plan every step with separation; impact ticks run hit tests; the spatial hash is rebuilt on the
  first tick that queries it. So p50 is the quiet tick and p99 the beat tick. The maxima are
  single outliers, likely preemption on the shared machine.
- TD12's line is p99 <= 3.5 ms in the player; here (dotnet, shared machine) the choreographer
  stays under it up to about N = 1,800.

The song's timeline
12,145 entities, 547,198 segments, 39,381 actions, 28,288 effects, 2,441 sounds (aggregated),
11,940 framing samples. Deaths/s are 0.92-1.08 of each section's load preset (tested: within
10%); worst build 0.921 and breakdown 0.936. Per section: song-summary.txt.
Not shown or not met:
- On-screen counts lag targets where the hero travels (drop-2 72%, final-peak 70% of target):
  the reserve walks in behind a moving view and a kill zone forms around the hero. Where targets
  fall (outro, ending) the horde lingers above them. TD13 tunes the level script.
- Hits/s fall short of the presets' 2x deaths in dense sections (final-peak 160 against 200):
  the hit shapes are capped at 2.5x and at a quarter of the view's width.
- The goal is reached in 6 of the 9 sections that name one.

Stress timelines and fill
At the tracked N = 1200 (the 40 m fill count): every bar's average on screen within 5% of N for
S1-S4, S5 averaging 87-89% (worst beats remove 30% of the screen every 4 bars), deaths/s within
1% of the preset. Mean on screen at higher N (tick-timing.md):
  N 2000:  S1 1861   S3 1571   S5 1119
  N 2800:  S1 1899   S3 1481   S5 1111
Monsters converge on the hero, so the crowd is a disc that never fills the view's far corners
(about 1,900 at most without kills), and the kill zone empties the middle. At N = 2000, S2
(combos only) deals 90% of its deaths and S5 79%. TD7's rows should carry the measured on-screen
count rather than assume N above about 1,500 at 40 m.
